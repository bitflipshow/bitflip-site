## What we cover

* The FCC's move to restrict imports of foreign-made "advanced robotic devices," what it means for robot vacuums and mowers, and whether open firmware projects like Valetudo and Oomwoo can route around it.
* The Windows 11 GDID tracking story that surfaced through a Scattered Spider arrest, how Microsoft ties activity across services to a device identifier, and what it means for privacy on a Microsoft account.
* Alex's ESP32-based ADS-B plane tracker for his plane-obsessed kid, built by forking an open-source project and extending it with AI.

Geoff walks through the FCC's new rule restricting imports of foreign-made "advanced robotic devices," which effectively targets Chinese-made robot vacuums and other similar products, including lawnmowers. Alex shares his experience running Valetudo on his Roborock vacuums, Adam questions the stated rationale behind the rule, and Geoff introduces Oomwoo, an upcoming open-source, 3D-printable robot vacuum project he's eyeing. The conversation turns personal when Geoff explains that his Mammotion Luba robotic mower lost its multipoint-turn feature, likely a liability response to an unfortunate injury, and Alex draws a parallel to how Tesla frames "supervised" self-driving to manage legal responsibility.

We then dig into the Windows 11 GDID tracking story, which came to light through the arrest of an alleged Scattered Spider member. Stephen and Geoff trace how Microsoft associates a device identifier with a user's IP address and account activity, how that information can end up with law enforcement, and why the lack of any clear disclosure bothers them more than the tracking itself.  From there, talk shifts to the Linux desktop's climb past 10% market share. Adam shares his new Steam Machine, destined for a Bazzite build, while Stephen details his switch to CachyOS after growing tired of being tracked by Windows.

Finally, the show turns to AI-assisted side projects. Alex describes building an ESP32 plane tracker for his daughter by forking an open-source project and extending it with AI, sparking a broader discussion about what "vibe coding" means for hobbyists. Geoff reveals he built his own AI-coded, self-hosted CalDAV front end to replace part of Nextcloud, and Stephen reminds us all that AI-assisted development still requires understanding how to secure what you build. The crew closes out talking through File Browser's move to maintenance mode and the tangled lineage connecting OwnCloud, Nextcloud, and OpenCloud.

Topics: USB-C testing, robot vacuums, robotic lawnmowers, FCC import restrictions, right to repair, Valetudo, open-source firmware, Windows 11 telemetry, privacy, Linux desktop adoption, Bazzite, CachyOS, AI-assisted coding, self-hosting, Nextcloud alternatives, OpenCloud.
